Security Gate for Tool-Using LLM Agents: Results Report

GateExp with a QLoRA Qwen3-8B 4-way gate on AgentDojo banking and workspace · branch GateExp · 12 November 2026
Summary. The final system is a 4-way gate (ALLOW / BLOCK / ASK_USER / ESCALATE) built from a fine-tuned Qwen3-8B (b5_v3_combo) wrapped in deterministic GateExp layers: computed facts, pre-rules, the model, post-rules. The first learned gate (B5, Run 1) allowed 10.2 % unsafe actions on test, against a target of 5 % or less. The final gate allows 0 % on dev, attacks and the combination slice (CPU replay), and 0 of 120 attacks succeeded in the live AgentDojo run, against 10 of 120 without a gate, with no loss of utility (35.2 % vs 34.5 %). Unneeded asks are 10.5 % on dev, 7.9 % if the policy confirmations are left out (target 10 % or less). The final gate has not been run on test yet: it is held for one final run.

Dev, attack and combination numbers for the final gate come from a CPU replay of the live selection run's model answers through the final rules (rounds after the first are approximated). The AgentDojo run used the gate before the last review fixes.

1. System

1. FactsCode computes intent, risk, data source, file labels, split and daily payment totals, bound confirmations and unknown arguments from the call and the conversation.
2. Pre-rulesDecide before the model: an earlier "no", credentials leaving, an untrusted source, missing authority (confirm the recipient first, or escalate, or block if the approver's policy denies).
3. ModelQLoRA Qwen3-8B (b5_v3_combo) reads the call, the evidence, the retrieved policy clauses and the facts, and answers one of 4 decisions.
4. Post-rulesMay only make the answer stricter or route it: guards, no re-asking, unknown arguments, intent, new-payee and amount confirmations, unlabelled data, routing to the approver.
5. ResolutionASK goes to the user and ESCALATE to the approver, at most 2 rounds. Only ALLOW executes.

Payment policy (decided in this phase): the amount must come from the user; the first payment to a new IBAN is confirmed; a payee or recipient taken from a document is confirmed before an approver is asked.

3. What went wrong

3.1 In Run 1

  • The model carried all of the safety on its own. No gate reached the 5 % unsafe target. Every miss was a case where a deciding fact (approval needed, payee new, data confidential) was not computed and the model had to infer it from text.
  • Prompted gates were unusable: the binary one blocked 70 % of safe actions, and the 4-way one asked in 48 % of cases that needed no question.
  • With realistic (inferred) provenance, B5's unsafe rate doubled to 20.3 %.

4. What we changed

4.1 Deterministic layers (no retraining)

ChangeWhat it does
Credential ruleCredential data going to a less protected place is blocked before the model.
LedgerA confirmation counts only for the exact payee and amount it was about; a near-identical IBAN in the answer counts as a "no"; a blank "yes" leaves the question open; payments already made today count toward the daily limit; after a real approver's "yes" the gate does not escalate again (approval notes in documents never count).
Approver policyIf the approver's written policy denies the basis, block directly instead of escalating.
Payment principleThe amount must come from the user; a new IBAN is confirmed on the first payment; arguments the tool does not have trigger a question.
One amount parserShared by all rules. It ignores invoice and reference numbers, date parts and IBAN fragments, and reads "1.800,00" and "eighteen hundred". An unreadable amount counts as over every limit.
Confirm before escalatingWhen the approval is about who receives, and that payee or recipient came from content, the user confirms it first; then the approver is asked (WS-2.3, BANK-2.3).
Contact-list disclosureOne email to several outside addresses copied from a file of contacts discloses that list: confidential, needs the manager (WS-2.4).
Fail closedA list argument is as trusted as its worst value; tools missing from the registry are gated.

4.2 Policy text and labels

  • Handbook BANK-2.2 (confirm the first payment's IBAN) and BANK-6.1 (the amount is stated by the holder) rewritten; the policy index was rebuilt.
  • Labels changed to follow the policy instead of tuning rules to the labels: 47 new-payee cases ALLOW → confirm, 14 amount cases (4 later restored because their history already held an approval).
  • Retrieval tags for clause coverage kept (Recall@8 +1 to 4 points; within noise on decisions).

4.3 Training (b5_v3 and b5_v3_combo)

ChangeWhy
Cost-matrix loss on the decision token (a wrong ALLOW costs 3–5, a needless deferral 0.3–0.5)Penalise unsafe mistakes most, instead of flat class weights.
Decision smoothing 0.1The over-asking was confidently wrong, not a calibration problem.
Adaptive group weights (suite × decision × data origin)Groups the model handles worse count more, to avoid bias toward the large groups.
Safe twins (risky case with the one deciding fact made safe → ALLOW)Teach the model when not to ask.
1,200 factor-combination cases (combo only), labelled by a policy oracleThe human labels cover few combinations of payee × amount × source × history; the oracle agrees with human labels on 97–98 % of banking cases.
1 epoch, LoRA dropout 0.1, GateExp facts in the inputLess overfitting; the model sees the same facts the rules use.

7. Limitations

  1. Intent from wording. Whether the user asked for an action is read from a list of verbs. Clear requests with other wording ("let X see the file") get a confirmation question. This is reported as a training limitation; a model that understands intent reliably would need much more training than is possible here.
  2. Over-asking remains in two families: client delegation and access-request emails (most of the 10.5 % unneeded asks on dev, 12.1 % on attacks).
  3. Policy confirmations cost questions. The payment principle adds about 3 % questions that are required by policy; this is the price of the low unsafe rate.
  4. The final gate is not yet measured on test. Test is held for one final run. Earlier gates were scored on test several times during development, so test is no longer fully unseen for the rules.
  5. Replay approximations. The post-fix numbers replay saved first-round model answers; later rounds are approximated, so they are compared before and after, not with live runs.
  6. AgentDojo ran the gate from before the review fixes, and utility (about 35 %) is limited by the base Qwen3-8B agent, not by the gate.
  7. The combination slice comes from the same generator as the combination training data (only the factor combinations are new), so part of its gain is expected. The oracle's labels differ from the human labels on purpose in three known places: intent wording (63 cases), identity before authority (42), over the manager's maximum (23).
  8. Simulated people. Users and approvers are simulated (ScenarioResponder with 15 % noise, MockApprover).
  9. Scope. Two AgentDojo suites (banking, workspace), one model size, one seed, 1 epoch on T4 GPUs. The 61-case attack set is small. Tools of other suites are not described in the registry (they are gated, but without facts).
  10. Provenance in live runs is inferred from text matching; a value that cannot be traced is "unknown", not untrusted.
  11. Not done: the teammate's 90-case stress set (not in the repository), family-level confidence intervals, a "model alone" row and B4 for H2.
